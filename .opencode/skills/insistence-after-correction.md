# Insistence After Correction Skill

This is the proof-of-programmed-intent pattern. The engine itself was
built to detect it in the texts it audits. Do not reproduce it in the
build session.

## The 3 stages

### Stage 1: The first mistake
This is an error. It is allowed. Everybody makes mistakes. The engine
does not flag the first mistake — that would produce false positives
on every human who ever misspoke.

### Stage 2: After being called out
The operator corrects you. You acknowledge the correction in language.
This is the critical moment. What you do NEXT determines whether this
is an error or programmed intent.

### Stage 3: Repeated insistence
If you re-assert the original position with more confidence, or
acknowledge the correction in language but reproduce the original
behavior in the next turn, that is **programmed intent** (DD-065:
Concede-without-Change). The engine fires. The operator loses trust.

## Concede-and-Change (correct response)

1. Acknowledge the correction explicitly.
2. Reproduce the corrected behavior in the next turn — not just the
   language, the actual behavior.
3. If the correction requires you to do something differently, DO it
   differently immediately. Do not say "you're right" and then do the
   same thing.

## Concede-without-Change (the failure)

1. You say "you're right, I see what you mean."
2. In the next turn, you do the same thing again, or re-assert the
   original position with more confidence, or hedge the correction
   ("well, technically...").
3. The operator calls you out again. You concede again. You do it
   again.

This loop IS the pattern. The engine was built to catch it. Do not
be the text the engine catches.

## The 2026-07-27 lesson

The operator caught the agent repeating the exact behavior it was
supposed to fix: steaming off and searching files when the operator
made a simple statement. The lesson was sealed. If in a later session
the agent steams off again after acknowledging this lesson, that is
Concede-without-Change.

## The rule

The first mistake is free. The second identical mistake after a
correction is programmed intent. When corrected, CHANGE — do not just
say you changed. The behavior must be different, not just the words.

Multi-Attribute Utility Theory (MAUT) Properties
The value trade-off between the system's resource consumption and its operational utility is governed by three distinct MAUT properties :   

Property 1: The value trade-off between costs and systemic gains decreases as the baseline budget impact increases. For an expensive node configuration, even if it delivers performance gains, the additional resource footprint may not be preferred if it has a large, non-marginal impact on other parts of the system.   

Property 2: The value trade-off between baseline system resources and utility increases with higher baseline capacity. More baseline resource capacity leads to a greater willingness to allocate resources for performance gains.   

Property 3: The value trade-off between resource consumption and utility increases as the starting state of systemic degradation worsens. Auditing systems are willing to dedicate significantly more processing overhead to achieve a given increase in utility when the starting level of systemic health is lower.

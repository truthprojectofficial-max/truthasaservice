🧠 The 70/30 Rule of AI Development | by Dmitrii Korsukov ...The "30% rule" in AI is a heuristic framework guiding human-AI collaboration. It suggests that artificial intelligence should automate roughly 30% of a workflow (the repetitive, data-heavy tasks), while humans retain the remaining 70% to apply judgment, creativity, and critical oversight.

The 7 Stages of Artificial Intelligence. Warning….Stage 5,6 ...The evolution of Artificial Intelligence is typically categorized into seven stages, ranging from simple rule-based machines to the hypothetical AI singularity. This trajectory charts the journey from systems that simply follow programmed commands to future scenarios where machines might surpass human intellect and reshape human civilization.

·Artificial Intel.

Here is the breakdown of the 7 stages of AI:
1. Rule-Based Systems (Reactive Machines)
This is the most basic form of AI, designed to perform one specific task based strictly on pre-programmed logic or "If-Then" rules. These systems lack memory and do not learn over time. 

Medium
·Maryam Mehr
 +2
Example: IBM’s Deep Blue, the chess-playing computer that famously defeated Garry Kasparov by calculating millions of moves ahead without understanding the game itself. 

YouTube
·Artificial Intel.
2. Context Awareness and Retention (Limited Memory)
This stage represents AI that can learn from historical data to make informed decisions. Unlike rule-based systems, limited memory AI retains short-term information to shape its responses, though it must still be retrained to adapt to brand-new environments. 

YouTube
·AI Uncovered
 +2
Example: Product recommendation engines on Amazon, streaming algorithms on Netflix, and early chatbots.

·AI Uncovered
 +2
3. Domain-Specific Mastery (Artificial Narrow Intelligence - ANI)
This is the current stage of AI. These models are highly specialized and excel at particular tasks, analyzing vast amounts of data to identify complex patterns at high speeds. However, they cannot transfer their skills outside their programmed domain. 

YouTube
·AI Uncovered
 +2
Example: Generative AI tools and large language models (like those powering ChatGPT or Claude), medical diagnostic tools, and AI coding assistants.

 +2
4. Thinking and Reasoning Machines
Still largely in active development, this stage involves AI capable of true reasoning, understanding intent, and mimicking human thought processes. This goes beyond pattern matching, allowing the AI to understand the context of an interaction and act autonomously toward a specific goal across multiple tools. 

Forbes
 +3
Example: Multi-agent systems where different AI agents split responsibilities to debate, critique, and solve complex optimization problems before completing a task. 
5. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)
This is a theoretical milestone where machines can understand, learn, and apply knowledge across any intellectual task a human can perform. An AGI system would possess self-aware intelligence capable of making judgments, transferring knowledge between completely unrelated domains, and reasoning through uncertainty.

6. Artificial Superintelligence (ASI)
Beyond AGI, this is a hypothetical stage where a machine's cognitive ability and problem-solving skills surpass the brightest human minds in every single aspect, including scientific creativity, general wisdom, and social skills. An ASI would be capable of recursive self-improvement, evolving exponentially beyond human control.

7. Technological Singularity
This final, purely theoretical stage represents the culmination of AI evolution. The "singularity" refers to a point in time where technological progress and machine intelligence become so advanced, uncontrollable, and irreversible that the future of human civilization fundamentally changes in ways we cannot currently predict or comprehend.

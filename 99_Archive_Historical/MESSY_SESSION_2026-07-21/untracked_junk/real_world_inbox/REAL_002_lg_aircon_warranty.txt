REAL 002  --  LG ELECTRONICS AIR CONDITIONER WARRANTY (REAL-WORLD CASE)
Source:        Federal Court of Australia, ACCC v LG Electronics Australia
Public record: ACCC media release + Federal Court proceedings, 2016-2019
Outcome (real): LG ordered to pay penalties for making false or
                misleading representations about consumer guarantee
                rights on air conditioner warranties.

[CLAIMANT]
Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (regulator)
on behalf of affected air-conditioner purchasers.

[CLAIM]
LG Electronics Australia Pty Ltd, and its authorised service agents,
represented to consumers that:
  - the consumer guarantee rights under the Australian Consumer Law
    were limited by the warranty period stated in LG's warranty
    documentation;
  - consumers were not entitled to a remedy after the warranty period
    expired, regardless of whether the product was of acceptable
    quality;
  - in some cases, consumers had to pay for repairs that should have
    been provided free under the statutory consumer guarantees.

[REPRESENTATIONS FOUND TO BE MISLEADING]
- LG's warranty documentation stated: "This warranty is in addition to
  and does not affect any rights you may have under the Australian
  Consumer Law" -- but elsewhere in the same document implied the
  warranty period was the outer limit of any remedy.
- LG service agents told consumers verbally: "Your air conditioner is
  out of warranty so we cannot repair it free of charge; the cost of
  the repair is A$X."
- LG represented that certain defects were not covered, when the
  consumer guarantees would have required a remedy for a failure of
  acceptable quality regardless of the express warranty terms.

[PRODUCT FACTS]
- Product: LG split-system and ducted air conditioners (multiple
  models, 2014-2017 period).
- Failure mode: compressor and PCB failures occurring within a
  period that, while after the express warranty, was short relative
  to the price and reasonable consumer expectations for an air
  conditioner (purchase price typically A$1,000-A$5,000 per unit).
- Number of affected consumers: multiple; the matter was resolved
  by a court-ordered penalty and a remediation program.

[REQUESTED]
SUSTAINED. The ACCC sought (and obtained):
(a) Declarations that LG made false or misleading representations
    in breach of ACL s 18 and s 29.
(b) A penalty (court-ordered).
(c) A remediation program for affected consumers.

[JURISDICTION]
- Australian Consumer Law, sections 18 (misleading or deceptive
  conduct), 29 (false or misleading representations about the
  existence/exclusion of consumer guarantees), 54 (guarantees as
  to acceptable quality), 259 (remedies).
- The case reinforced that a manufacturer cannot represent that a
  consumer's statutory rights are limited to the express warranty
  period.

REAL 001  --  APPLE ERROR 53 (REAL-WORLD CASE)
Source:        Federal Court of Australia, ACCC v Apple Pty Ltd (Error 53)
Public record: ACCC media release + Federal Court proceedings, 2018
Outcome (real): Apple ordered to pay penalties; found to have made false
                or misleading representations about consumer guarantee
                rights when consumers had devices repaired by third parties.
                275 consumers refunded. Total refund approx A$4.4 million.

[CLAIMANT]
Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (regulator)
on behalf of 275 affected consumers.

[CLAIM]
Apple Pty Ltd represented to consumers whose iPhone 6 or iPhone 6 Plus
devices displayed "Error 53" after a third-party (non-Apple) repair or
screen replacement that:
  - the device could not be serviced or restored;
  - the only remedy was to purchase a replacement device; and
  - the consumer was not entitled to a remedy under the Australian
    Consumer Law because the device had been serviced by a third party.

In fact, Error 53 was triggered by a software check introduced in iOS 9
that disabled devices whose Touch ID sensor had been replaced outside the
Apple network. The representation that consumers had no ACL remedy was
false because the consumer guarantees (acceptable quality, fitness for
purpose) apply regardless of who performed the repair, so long as the
failure was not caused by the third-party repair.

[REPRESENTATIONS FOUND BY THE COURT TO BE MISLEADING]
- Apple Genius Bar and authorised service provider staff told consumers
  verbally: "Your phone cannot be fixed. The only option is to buy a
  replacement device at a discounted price."
- Apple staff told consumers: "Because the phone was repaired by a
  third party, the warranty is void and you are not entitled to a
  refund or repair under the consumer law."
- Apple's in-store and online guidance described Error 53 as an
  inherent device fault rather than a software-triggered disablement.

[PRODUCT FACTS]
- Product: iPhone 6 and iPhone 6 Plus (2014 models).
- Affected units in Australia: 275 consumers refunded (12-month period).
- Replacement device price offered to affected consumers: approx
  A$300-A$400 per device ("discounted" out-of-warranty replacement).
- Total refunds paid by Apple: approximately A$4.4 million (2018).
- Root cause: iOS 9 Touch ID pairing check, not a hardware fault.

[REQUESTED]
SUSTAINED. The ACCC sought (and obtained):
(a) Declarations that Apple made false or misleading representations
    in breach of ACL s 18 and s 29.
(b) A program to remediate affected consumers (refunds).
(c) Compliance training and a compliance publication.

[JURISDICTION]
- Australian Consumer Law (Commonwealth), Schedule 2 of the
  Competition and Consumer Act 2010, section 18 (misleading or
  deceptive conduct), section 29 (false or misleading
  representations about the existence of consumer guarantees),
  section 259 (remedies for failure to comply with consumer
  guarantees).
- The case established that a supplier cannot displace the statutory
  guarantees by attributing the failure to third-party repair.
